# Chicago Regional Transit System Context

This note provides background for readers of the NITA website audit. It explains the relationship between the regional authority, the three transit operators, and the two websites in scope.

## How the agencies fit together

As of September 1, 2026, the **Northern Illinois Transit Authority (NITA)** replaced the former **Regional Transportation Authority (RTA)**. NITA leads regional transit policy and investment, coordinates planning across the region, and oversees funding and system performance. It sets regional priorities for fares, service coordination, safety, capital planning, accessibility, and customer information.

NITA is the regional authority; it does not operate all buses and trains directly. The **Chicago Transit Authority (CTA)**, **Metra**, and **Pace** continue to operate transit services and maintain their systems. NITA's current budget describes these agencies as the region's “Service Boards.”

| Organization | Main role | In plain language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **NITA** — Northern Illinois Transit Authority | Regional policy, planning, funding, and coordination | Sets shared direction and helps coordinate the regional system. |
| **CTA** — Chicago Transit Authority | Bus and rapid-transit rail service in Chicago and nearby communities | Operates city buses and the elevated/subway rail network known as the “L.” |
| **Metra** | Regional commuter rail | Operates trains connecting Chicago with communities across the suburbs. |
| **Pace** — Pace Suburban Bus | Suburban bus and related services | Operates suburban fixed-route buses, On Demand, Dial-a-Ride, and ADA paratransit services. |

The NITA region covers six northeastern Illinois counties: **Cook, DuPage, Kane, Lake, McHenry, and Will**. Some rail service extends beyond Illinois; for example, the South Shore Line is a separate transit partner that connects Chicago with Indiana.

## Distinguishing the programs

The two websites in this audit serve different purposes:

- **[nita.illinois.gov](https://nita.illinois.gov/)** is the public website for the regional authority. It covers regional planning, funding, rider information, and programs such as reduced or free fares.
- **[mytransitbenefit.com](https://www.mytransitbenefit.com/)** explains the NITA Transit Benefit Fare Program, an employer-sponsored pre-tax benefit for eligible commuting and parking expenses. It has separate information for employers and employees.

The Transit Benefit Fare Program is distinct from **Access**, **Reduced Fare**, and **Ride Free** programs. Those programs provide fare assistance based on their respective eligibility rules; they are not the same as an employer's pre-tax commuter benefit.

## Why this matters for the website audit

- Evaluate each website according to its audience and purpose. NITA's site serves the broader regional public, riders, municipalities, and transit partners; My Transit Benefit primarily serves employers and participating employees.
- Do not assume NITA directly operates CTA, Metra, or Pace services. Attribute service details to the operating agency where appropriate.
- Treat old references to “RTA” with context. NITA is the current regional authority, but legacy materials, brands, and program names may still appear during the transition.
- Keep employer commuter benefits separate from low-income, senior, disability, or other reduced-fare programs when reviewing content and search intent.
