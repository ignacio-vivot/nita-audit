# 
 Access Program

The Northern Illinois Transit Authority (NITA) is proud to provide reduced fares to low income transit riders.

The program, which started in 2024, expanded regionwide in 2026 and allows residents of the NITA region who qualify for the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) or Illinois Department of Human Services' FRESH Program to take CTA, Metra, or Pace at a reduced price. Applications are available at [GetAccess.org](http://GetAccess.org).  

The Access Program is an expansion of NITA's [Ride Free and Reduced Fare](https://nita.illinois.gov/riders/free-and-reduced-fare-programs) programs, which will continue to be available to older adults and individuals with disabilities.

## Who is eligible?

The Access Program is available to anyone living in a household in the NITA region (Cook, DuPage, Lake, Kane, McHenry, and Will counties) who qualifies for the Supplemental Nutritional Assistance Program (SNAP) or IDHS' FRESH Program. SNAP is targeted to households with low income (165% of poverty level). Learn more about [SNAP eligibility](https://www.dhs.state.il.us/page.aspx?item=30357) in Illinois.

## How do I get Access?

The fastest way to get a permit is to apply online at [GetAccess.org](http://GetAccess.org). You can also call NITA Customer Service at (312) 913-3110. [Paper applications](https://nita.illinois.gov/riders/travel-assistance-digital-library) are available to download, fill out, and mail in. Those wishing to apply in person can do so at 62 registration sites. View a [map of registration sites](https://rtagis.maps.arcgis.com/apps/instant/nearby/index.html?appid=85e194b8baf940b2afc8e510d86f8699) to find one close to you.

Once found eligible, you will receive an Access Permit in 7 to 10 business days. To ride Metra, you can purchase a reduced fare ticket and show your permit to the conductor. You can tap your permit to pay to ride CTA and Pace at the reduced fare.

## Frequently Asked Questions

 How is this different than the current NITA Reduced Fare program?

NITA's current Reduced Fare program is a federally mandated program providing approximately half-priced fare across all three regional transit operators – CTA, Metra, and Pace – for people with disabilities and adults 65 and older. The Ride Free program is a State of Illinois-mandated, means-tested program providing free public transportation for people with disabilities and adults 65 and older who qualify for the Illinois Department on Aging’s Benefit Access Program. NITA operates the eligibility process for both fare programs.

Eligibility for the Access Program is determined based on a person being a member of a household that receives Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) benefits or IDHS FRESH Program benefits.

 Is the program available on CTA and Pace?

Thanks to the NITA Act, the Access Pilot Program expanded regionwide to CTA and Pace on October 1, 2026, and continues to be available for Metra.

 Who is paying for the Access Program?

As of 2026, the program is being funded by new money from the NITA Act.
