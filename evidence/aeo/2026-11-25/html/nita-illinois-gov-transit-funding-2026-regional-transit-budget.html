2026 Regional Transit Operating Budget and Capital Program

The RTA reviews, adopts, and monitors the annual operating budgets and five-year capital programs of all three Service Boards (CTA, Metra, and Pace).  

The RTA Board is considering a proposed amendment to the 2026 Operating Budget. Read more.

RTA board considering proposed amendment to the 2026 Operating Budget

Late last year, Governor JB Pritzker signed the Northern Illinois Transit Authority (NITA) Act, a historic transit funding and reform package that will bring an estimated $1.2 billion in new annual operating funding to the system. 

The RTA, CTA, Metra, and Pace are requesting input on a proposed amendment to the 2026 Operating Budget to allocate funding to key rider-focused improvements in alignment with the NITA Act. Learn more about what this proposed amendment would fund—including transit safety and security enhancements, improved service, affordable fare programs, and more—by watching a presentation or reading an RTA blog post.
 

2026 Budget reflects a new era for transit

The 2026 Regional Transit Operating Budget and Five-Year Capital Program (and Capital Program Appendix) comes shortly after the Illinois General Assembly’s October 31 vote to approve Senate Bill 2111 – a significant transit funding and reform package that will bring $1.2 billion in new annual operating funding to the system – marks a historic investment that will protect the essential service of today and lay the foundation for the transit of tomorrow. 

Preparation of the 2026 budget came as the region stared down an operating fiscal cliff that threatened the system’s future, but the new legislation, signed by Governor JB Pritzker on December 16, means riders will not experience service cuts or fare increases in 2026 and instead will benefit from continued improvements to frequency, reliability, and safety.

NITA Act means no fare increase or service cuts for riders

In December 2025, Governor JB Pritzker signed the Northern Illinois Transit Authority (NITA) Act into a law. The legislation includes an estimated $1.2 billion in new annual operating funding for CTA, Metra, and Pace. This sustainable funding means riders will not experience service cuts or fare increases in 2026 and instead provides a foundation to improve frequency, reliability, and safety for millions of riders in the coming years. The proposed budgets below were submitted before the NITA Act was signed and will be revised before the final budget passage in December 2025. Read more about what it means for riders in a blog post.

2026 Budget Schedule

After CTA, Metra, and Pace each submit their budgets, the RTA as the oversight agency, works to develop a Regional Transit Operating Budget and Capital Program to be released for public comment and voted on by the RTA board in December.

Timeline

Development of the 2026 Operating Budget, Two-Year Financial Plan, and Five-Year Capital program takes place over several months.

  • May: RTA Board adopts annual budget call
  • July: Release of preparatory funding amounts
  • August: RTA Board adopts proposed funding amounts
  • October: Service boards submit proposed budgets and capital programs to the RTA
  • October-December: RTA and the Service Boards hold public hearings and give presentations to county board meetings
  • November 15: Statutory deadline for Service Boards to submit adopted budgets
  • November 20: Service Board present their budgets to the RTA Board of Directors
  • December 4: RTA Virtual Public Hearing. 
  • December 18: RTA Board of Directors considers regional budget for adoption
